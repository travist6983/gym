# Story options: one hero object, one journey down the page

**Content basis:** the homepage on its own is one screen, so every option brings verbatim copy from the About Us, Training, Memberships, Reviews and FAQ pages onto the homepage (see `content.json`). Nothing is invented.

---

## A. "One Session": a member moves through the gym (your hint) **RECOMMENDED**

**The object:** one athlete figure, drawn as an original SVG silhouette in black with a brand-red rim light. Straight down the page, it:

| # | Section | What the member does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hero | Walks up to the door in the dark and taps a key fob. The hex ceiling lights snap on: "OPEN 24/7 FOR HARDWORX GYM MEMBERS". JOIN NOW stays in the sticky nav from here on. |
| 2 | Mission | Walks straight onto a free machine: "no longer waiting for equipment". A "500" counter ticks up to the membership cap as the floor stays uncrowded. |
| 3 | Facilities | Uses the equipment in order: cardio warm-up, pin-stack machine, plate-loaded press, free weights. Each piece of equipment is named from their copy as the member uses it, and the equipment brands run in a marquee. |
| 4 | Training | A phone UI next to the member logs each set and rep as it happens ("log their weights and repetitions to keep track of progress"). |
| 5 | Recovery | Infrared sauna glow, then cold plunge with frost. Ends on "All Included in the Membership Price!" |
| 6 | Memberships | A light panel slides up over the dark page and the prices count up: $79, $850, $150. The auto-renew notice is shown in full. |
| 7 | Reviews and updates | Three real 5-star reviews, plus the merch and legs-section cards. |
| 8 | FAQ | Locker room, with "6 private restrooms and 3 showers" counted on scroll. The guest-policy notice is styled as a notice. |
| 9 | Contact and footer | Address, staffed hours and the form. The member hits a final double-biceps flex, and the silhouette morphs into the client's flexing-arm "H" logo. |

**Why it fits:**
- The client sells a session without friction: "maximize their workout in a timely manner", "no longer waiting for equipment".
- Their app is about "progress".
- Their facilities list reads like a circuit, and their logo is literally a flexed arm.

**The moment people react to:** the last flex turning into their own H logo.

**On your hint about losing weight and gaining muscle:**
- It fits the progress theme.
- But their copy never promises weight loss, and showing a heavy figure turning thin can read as body-shaming or as an implied result.
- I'd show strength building instead (shoulders, arms and posture fill out across the stages) and keep body-size change subtle. You choose below.

**Risks:**
- An animated human figure is the hardest thing here to render well. A stiff figure looks cheap.
- The plan to avoid that is about 8 hand-drawn key poses with morphing between them, not a full skeleton.
- Mobile works well: the member travels down a centre lane.

## B. "The Red Plate": one 45 lb plate travels down the page

**The object:** a red bumper plate, matching their red machines. It:
1. rolls out of the hero
2. drops onto a plate-loaded machine (the press moves as you scroll)
3. slides onto a barbell for a scrubbed lift
4. stacks with others into three piles for $79, $850 and $150
5. rolls into the rack at the bottom, where the rack spells the H

**Why it fits:** reviews mention "Lots of plates for free weights". The facility is pin-stack and plate-loaded.

**The moment people react to:** the plate slamming onto the bar and the bar lifting as you scroll.

**Risks:** no person, so less emotion. It drops your hint. It's the easiest of the three to render and the best on mobile.

---

## C. "Around the Clock": scrolling is 24 hours

**The object:** a glowing key fob or clock hand. The whole page is one day, midnight to midnight:
- The fob opens the door at 3 AM.
- Staffed hours (10–8, Fri 10–3) light up as the dial passes them.
- The guest policy appears at the "non-staffed" hours.
- Recovery happens in the evening.
- The day ends at the contact section.

**Why it fits:** "OPEN 24/7" is their H1, and the gap between 24/7 access and staffed hours is the thing buyers most often get wrong (it's an FAQ answer).

**The moment people react to:** the hex ceiling lights snapping on when the fob taps at 3 AM.

**Risks:** abstract, with no body or effort on screen. Clever but less emotional.

---

## Ranking

1. **A**, with strength gain as the change and a small 24-hour clock in the corner borrowed from C, so "open at 3 AM, no waiting" still lands.
2. **B**
3. **C**
